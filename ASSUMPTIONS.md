# Assumptions, parameters and where they come from

Every number the model uses, what justifies it, and what happens if it is wrong.
Written because a demonstrator whose assumptions are undocumented is not a
demonstrator — it is a black box that happens to produce plausible numbers.

Nothing here is calibrated against proprietary data. Values are either standard
engineering figures, or order-of-magnitude representative of the French market
and labelled as such.

---

## 1. Market data — **synthetic**

Generated by `market_data.py` from a seeded RNG. Not sourced from RTE or ENTSO-E.

| Feature | Value / shape | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| Base spot | 78 EUR/MWh | Order of magnitude for recent French day-ahead |
| Diurnal shape | morning peak ~08h, evening peak ~19h30, solar depression ~13h30, night trough | Standard load-minus-solar net demand profile |
| Weekend | ×0.86 | Lower industrial demand |
| Residual | AR(1), φ=0.75, σ=9 | Hourly prices are strongly autocorrelated; white noise would be unrealistic |
| Scarcity spikes | +120…260 EUR/MWh, ~1 per 60 h, 2 h duration | Gives the battery and the CCGT something worth reacting to |
| Negative episode | −70…110 EUR/MWh, 3 h | Solar/wind surplus; also exercises the charge/discharge exclusivity binary |
| Gas | 30 EUR/MWh_gas, shallow random walk | TTF order of magnitude; fuel moves slowly relative to power |
| CO2 | 70 EUR/t, shallow random walk | EU ETS order of magnitude |
| FCR capacity | 12 + 14×tightness EUR/MW/h | Within historically observed French FCR range (has spanned ~5–40) |
| aFRR up capacity | 9 + 16×tightness EUR/MW/h | Same basis |
| aFRR down capacity | 7 + 6×(1−tightness) EUR/MW/h | Down reserve is typically cheaper than up |
| aFRR up activation | 1.25 × spot + 15 | Upward regulation energy clears at a premium to spot |
| aFRR down activation | 0.85 × spot | Downward regulation energy is bought back slightly below spot |

**Sensitivity.** The reserve capacity price level drives the headline uplift
almost linearly. Halving it roughly halves the uplift. The *mechanism* — that
co-optimization dominates sequential optimization — does not depend on the level.

**Reserve prices are correlated with, not derived from, the spot.** They clear in
separate auctions. Modelling them as a deterministic function of spot would
manufacture a relationship the optimizer could then exploit.

## 2. BESS

| Parameter | Default | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| Power / capacity | 50 MW / 100 MWh | 2 h duration, typical of grid-scale BESS built for FCR+arbitrage |
| Charge / discharge efficiency | 0.94 / 0.94 | ~88% round trip, representative of Li-ion at the AC point |
| Degradation cost | 2 EUR/MWh discharged | Stands in for calendar + cycle ageing. **Without it the model over-cycles** on spreads a real operator would never chase |
| SOC bounds | 0 – nameplate | Simplification: real operation reserves margins at both ends |
| Initial / final SOC | 50% / 50% | Final SOC constraint stops the model "cheating" by emptying the battery on the last period with no obligation to refill |
| Reserve sustain duration | 0.25 h (FCR), 1.0 h (aFRR) | FCR prequalification in continental Europe is built on 15-min full activation; aFRR requirements differ by TSO |

**Sensitivity.** `sustain_duration` is the single most consequential BESS
assumption: it converts reserve MW into an SOC reservation. Raising FCR from
0.25 h to 1 h quadruples the energy a given reserve volume locks up and can
make reserve provision infeasible at low SOC.

## 3. CCGT

| Parameter | Default | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| Pmin / Pmax | 100 / 400 MW | 25% minimum stable load, typical for a large CCGT |
| Heat rate | 2.0 MWh_gas / MWh_e | 50% LHV efficiency. Modelled as **constant** — a real plant's heat rate is convex in load and would need a piecewise-linear formulation |
| CO2 factor | 0.202 t/MWh_gas | Standard natural-gas emission factor |
| Variable O&M | 3 EUR/MWh | Typical CCGT non-fuel variable cost |
| Startup cost | 15,000 EUR | Lumped hot/warm/cold start. Real startup cost depends on downtime — a three-state formulation would capture that |
| Ramp | ±200 MW/h | Relaxed on start/stop periods (see below) |
| Min up / min down | 4 h / 3 h | Thermal cycling limits |

Resulting SRMC at gas 30 / CO2 70: **2×30 + 2×0.202×70 + 3 = 91.28 EUR/MWh**.
This figure is asserted in `test_marginal_cost_matches_hand_calculation`, so the
cost stack cannot drift silently.

**Known simplification — startup trajectory.** Ramp constraints are relaxed on
the start and stop periods, so the unit reaches Pmin instantly. A real start is a
multi-hour sequence (purge, ignition, loading) during which the unit consumes
fuel and produces little. This makes the model *optimistic* about how quickly the
CCGT can capture a price spike.

## 4. Reserve products

**Capacity and activation are never conflated.** They are separate fields with
separate units and separate economics:

- `capacity_price_eur_mw_h` — paid for *availability*, earned whether or not the
  TSO calls.
- `activation_price_eur_mwh` — paid for *energy delivered*, only if called.

Because activation is unknown at bidding time, the optimizer values it through
`expected_activation_ratio` (default 0.12 up / 0.10 down) and the backtest
re-values against a drawn realized ratio.

**Activation is valued at its margin, not its gross price.** See README. This is
the assumption most likely to be got wrong elsewhere, and it changes the answer
by a lot: valuing activation gross inflated the portfolio uplift from ~93% to
~384% in an earlier iteration of this model, purely by treating delivered energy
as costless.

**Symmetric products are energy-neutral in expectation.** FCR is called up about
as often as down, so the energy legs cancel and only capacity remains. Consistent
with FCR being capacity-remunerated only in France.

**Volume caps** (FCR 25 MW, aFRR 100 MW per asset) proxy market depth and
prequalification. French FCR is ~600 MW system-wide; without a cap the model
sells every available MW into a market that could not absorb it. A proper
treatment bids into a supply curve rather than against a hard cap.

## 5. Portfolio

**The two assets are optimized independently and aggregated.** There is no
constraint coupling them. This is honest about what it is: portfolio *reporting*,
not portfolio *optimization*. A joint constraint — one aggregated reserve
obligation delivered collectively, a shared grid connection, a risk budget —
would change the solution, and is the main structural extension.

The energy-only vs energy+reserves comparison holds assets, horizon and prices
fixed and varies **only** market access, so the uplift is attributable to
co-optimization rather than to a change of assumptions.

## 6. Backtest

| Assumption | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Dispatch | **Fixed.** Never re-optimized with hindsight |
| Realized prices | Forecast + AR(1) error (φ=0.8, σ configurable) + occasional spikes |
| Realized activation ratio | Beta draw around the expected ratio — bursty, not Gaussian, because activation is bursty in reality |
| Realized activation price | Recomputed from realized spot |
| Imbalance settlement | **Not modelled.** Under/over-delivery versus the bid is valued at the realized activation price, not at a TSO-exact imbalance settlement |

`forecast_error_cost = expected_pnl − realized_pnl` is therefore the cost of
being wrong about the future with the strategy held fixed — not a full
attribution, which would separate price error from activation error from
imbalance exposure.

## 7. Numerical

- Solver: **HiGHS** via Pyomo APPSI. MIT-licensed, prebuilt wheel, no commercial
  licence — the whole stack installs with pip and runs in CI and in a free-tier
  container.
- Solutions are proven optimal; `solver.py` raises rather than returning a
  silently infeasible or suboptimal result.
- Schedules are rounded at 1e-9 and clipped at zero to strip solver residue
  before any downstream threshold or assertion sees them.
- Bid volumes below `min_volume_mw` (0.1 MW) are dropped, so numerical residue
  never becomes a bid line.
